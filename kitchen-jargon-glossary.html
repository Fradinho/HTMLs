The Bear — Season 1 & 2

Kitchen Jargon
Glossary

Every term screamed across the pass — decoded.

20 terms

Yes, Chef Hierarchy

The universal acknowledgment in a professional kitchen. When a chef gives an instruction, the only acceptable reply. Signals respect for the hierarchy, confirms the order was heard, and commits you to executing it.

"Fire table four." — "Yes, Chef."

Heard Communication

Shorter version of "Yes, Chef" used peer-to-peer or when volume makes full phrases hard. Confirms you received information without adding noise. In The Bear, Carmy uses it constantly to acknowledge Marcus or Sydney.

"Two more salmon on the fly." — "Heard."

Behind / Corner Safety

"Behind" is called out whenever you walk behind another cook to prevent collisions with knives or hot pans. "Corner" is shouted before turning a blind corner. These calls are non-negotiable safety protocol — ignoring them can cause serious injury.

"Corner!" before rounding the expeditor's station.

Mise en Place meez-ahn-PLASS Prep

French for "everything in its place." All ingredients chopped, measured, and arranged before cooking begins. A cook's mise defines their skill and discipline. In The Bear, Carmy is obsessive about it — it's practically a philosophy of life.

"Your mise is a mess. You'll never survive service like this."

Fire Service

The command to start cooking a dish right now. When the expo calls "fire," the clock starts. Not to be confused with a literal fire — though in The Bear, both happen.

"Fire two ribeyes, one chicken, heard?"

All Day Service

The running total of a specific item needed across all current tickets. Lets cooks batch efficiently instead of cooking one at a time. "Three salmon — two all day" means you need two total, not five.

"I've got four steaks all day, fire two now."

In the Weeds State

Being overwhelmed and behind — orders piling up faster than you can execute them. A cook in the weeds risks bringing down the whole pass. The Bear's entire first season is essentially one long stretch in the weeds.

"I'm in the weeds, I need a hand on sauté right now."

86 Inventory

An item is 86'd when it's sold out or unavailable. Also used as a verb for removing something (or someone). Origin debated — possibly from Delmonico's in New York, possibly military. Either way, when Richie says it, it's final.

"We're 86 on the lamb chops for the night."

On the Fly Urgency

Something needed immediately, usually to fix a mistake or satisfy a VIP guest. An "on the fly" order jumps the queue and adds pressure to an already busy station. Triggers anxiety in every cook who hears it.

"Table seven's entrée was wrong — I need a new one on the fly."

Expo / Expeditor Role

The person who runs the pass — calling out tickets, coordinating timing across stations, and ensuring every dish on a table leaves together. Often the head chef or sous chef. In The Bear, Carmy frequently expos, which shows how hands-on he is.

"Expo is calling ticket 12 — where's my risotto?"

The Pass Anatomy

The long counter between the kitchen and the front of house where finished plates are placed and inspected before going to the table. Nothing leaves the kitchen without passing through the pass. It's the last quality checkpoint.

"Get those plates to the pass — table three has been waiting."

Ticket / Dupe Operations

The printed or written order that comes from the front of house. "Dupe" (duplicate) is an older term for the carbon-copy chit. In The Bear, tickets pile up dramatically during chaotic service — a visual symbol of the restaurant's dysfunction.

"New ticket: two chicken, one veal, one vegetarian — fire it."

Family Meal Culture

A meal cooked by the kitchen staff for the entire restaurant team before service starts. Builds morale and camaraderie. In The Bear, Marcus's family meal experiments are a vehicle for his creativity — and Carmy's eventual mentorship of him.

"Marcus, you're on family meal tonight. Impress us."

Sous Chef SOO-shef Role

French for "under chef." The second-in-command, responsible for day-to-day kitchen operations, staff management, and stepping in when the head chef is absent. Sydney becomes sous chef in Season 2, a central arc of the show.

"Sydney's the sous chef — what she says goes when I'm not here."

Chef de Partie shef-duh-par-TEE Role

French for "chief of the party." A cook who is in charge of a specific station — sauté, grill, pastry, etc. Abbreviated CDP. Each station chef is responsible for their section's output and a small team of cooks beneath them.

"Tina's CDP on cold apps now — show her some respect."

Sauté Station saw-TAY Station

The stovetop-heavy station handling pan-seared proteins and sauces. Typically the most demanding and prestigious station in the kitchen — the final proving ground for serious line cooks. Fast, hot, and unforgiving.

"You want sauté? You better be ready to move."

Garde Manger gard-mahn-ZHAY Station

The cold kitchen station — responsible for salads, cold appetizers, charcuterie, and plated desserts in some kitchens. "Pantry" in casual speech. Often seen as an entry-level station, though skilled garde manger work is an art in itself.

"Cold apps are stacking up — someone get on garde manger."

Covers Operations

The number of individual diners served during a meal period. A high cover count means a busy night; chefs track covers per hour to gauge kitchen performance. "We did 180 covers last night" is a statement of pride — or exhaustion.

"We've got 120 covers tonight — everyone stays sharp."

Push / Service Service

"Service" is the entire dinner (or lunch) period when the restaurant is open and cooking for guests. "Push" refers to the intense peak rush within service when tickets flood in at once. The Bear renders the push as practically traumatic.

"Alright, service starts in ten — let's push."

Tasting Menu Concept

A fixed multi-course menu in which the chef decides every dish — guests surrender control to the kitchen's vision. Prestige restaurants live and die by their tasting menus. In The Bear Season 2, converting to a tasting menu format is the central creative tension as Carmy tries to build something world-class.

"We're not a beef-and-sauce spot anymore — we're doing a tasting menu."
